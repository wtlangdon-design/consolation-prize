Image 1 is a finished night map from my pixel-art adventure game, in the middle of a light grey canvas: a top-down, slightly angled drawn plan of a gold-rush town in a valley (left of centre) and the diggings beyond it (right), with roads, a creek, bridges, a fenced graveyard at the top, and mine workings. Image 2 is the coach stop where the game begins and ends, seen from the ground: a fenced stop with a blank signboard, a woodpile and a wagon, on a mountain road high above the town's lamps. The other images are references for the game's style only; do not copy anything from them into the map.

Make exactly these changes to Image 1, in its own night palette, pixel grid and scale:
1. A MOUNTAIN PASS AT THE TOP. Along the top edge of the map, above the town and to the left of the graveyard, draw a ridge of dark mountains with a notch, a pass, in it. Partway down the slope, on a flat shoulder overlooking the town, put the coach stop from Image 2, small: a little fenced yard beside the road with a woodpile and a wagon.
2. MOUNTAINS AROUND THE VALLEY. Along the left edge of the map, and along the top edge to the left of the pass, put dark mountain slopes and peaks, so the town sits in a valley ringed by mountains. The trees at the foot of them stay.
3. THE WEST ROAD GOES. The road that leaves the square's west end and runs off the left edge of the map is removed; grass and trees take its place. The road around the square itself stays.

Change nothing else. The town, its streets and buildings and lit windows, the three houses, the creek, every bridge, the graveyard and its road, the road east to the diggings, the hills, the mine workings and the lanterns stay exactly where they are and exactly as they are drawn.
