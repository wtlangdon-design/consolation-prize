Image 1 is the layout for one room of my pixel-art adventure game: grey blocks for walls and furniture, and the hero (the man in the long coat) standing on the floor. Paint this room as a finished game background with two people in it, keeping the camera, the layout and every block where image 1 puts it. Paint the room ONLY inside the darker middle band of image 1, edge to edge; leave the light grey strips above and below it flat light grey, untouched. The whole room, from the ceiling to the floor in front of the hero, fits inside that band.

THE ROOM: the claims registrar's office in a gold-rush town, 1850s, at night. A government office where every claim in the district is filed, in one book, in one hand. Orderly, plain, a little too official for the town around it. Image 5 is another office in the same town at the same hour, for the world and the night light only.
- Left wall: the street door, plain, with a small brass bell on a curled spring above it.
- Back wall, left: one window, dark night outside. Under it a long plain bench along the wall, the seat worn pale at the end nearest the window.
- Back wall, middle: a big map of mining claims pinned flat, every claim a small numbered rectangle, with NO readable writing; below it a wooden rack of pigeonhole slots holding printed forms.
- Right half: a long wooden counter, built a little too high, with a brass rail along its front and a band worn pale along its top. On top of the counter, a wood-and-glass partition with one open window in the middle where the registrar works. On the counter: a big ledger lying open, a pen on a long chain, a small brass desk bell, a neat stack of printed forms, a thick statute book. At the counter's left end a ticket spindle with numbered paper tickets on it.
- Behind the partition: a plain office clock on the wall, and at the right a heavy locked door with a printed notice posted beside it.
- In front of the counter: two short roped queue lines on brass posts, each with a small sign on a stand; both lines empty. The signs carry blank boards, NO letters.
- Floor: worn pine boards.
- Light: two oil lamps at the counter light the registrar's window and the floor in front of it; the rest of the room is dimmer. No daylight.

THE PEOPLE (this picture is a reference painting, so people are allowed in it):
- The hero from image 4, standing where image 1 puts him, at exactly that size, facing right toward the counter.
- REGISTRAR SUMP, standing behind the counter in the partition's open window, facing out toward the hero: a territorial clerk of the 1850s, middle-aged, clean-shaven, thin, upright, hair combed flat, small round spectacles, white shirt with black sleeve protectors over the forearms, a dark waistcoat. One hand rests flat on the open ledger. The counter hides him below the waist. He is the size of a standing man just behind the counter, a little smaller than the hero.

THE LOOK: the same pixel-art style as images 2, 3 and 6, and the same hand as the hero: flat, soft shading, very little texture, simple shapes, a visible pixel grid. Not sharp, not finely detailed, no hard internal contrast. Draw the registrar exactly as softly as the hero. This room has its own colours: pale institutional wood, paper and dust, mid values, not the all-brown of a saloon. Nothing near black where the hero stands.

RULES: no text, letters or numbers anywhere -- the map, the signs, the forms and the ledger carry marks, never words. No border, no frame, no caption.
