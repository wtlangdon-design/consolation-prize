THE ROOM: the lobby of the one hotel in a gold-rush town, 1850s, at night. Papered walls over dark wainscot, a plank floor with a worn runner; respectable on the cheap.
- Far left: the street door, closed.
- Left: the front window onto the dark street; under it a horsehair settee; beside it a brass spittoon.
- Left of middle, on the wall: a small oil painting of a mountain scene, and beside it an engraving of a bay with a smoking volcano.
- Middle: an upright piano in the corner under a potted fern -- the lobby's "parlor".
- Right of middle: the clerk's desk, with a big open register and a small brass bell on it; behind it on the wall a rack of key hooks, nearly all keys hanging; beside the rack a tall wall clock.
- Right: an iron stove.
- Far right: a staircase going up to the rooms, carpeted on its first four treads and bare above.
- Light: oil lamps on the walls and the stove; warm, the corners dim.

THE PEOPLE (this picture is a reference painting, so people are allowed in it):
- The hero from image 4, standing where image 1 puts him, at exactly that size, facing right toward the desk.
- THE HOTEL CLERK, behind the desk, seen from the waist up over it, reading the register: a neat, fussy man of about forty, hair parted in the centre and oiled flat, small round spectacles, a white shirt with black sleeve garters, a buttoned waistcoat, a bow tie; proprietorial and unhurried. The size of a standing man at that depth.

THE LOOK: the same pixel-art style as images 2, 3 and 5, and the same hand as the hero: the SAME BIG SQUARE PIXELS as the hero -- the clerk drawn on exactly the hero's coarse pixel grid, no finer -- flat, soft shading, very little texture, simple shapes. Warm browns, faded green paper, brass and lamplight. Nothing near black where the hero stands.
