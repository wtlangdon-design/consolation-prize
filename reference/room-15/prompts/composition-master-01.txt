Paint this room as a finished game background with people and animals in it, keeping the camera, the layout and every block where image 1 puts it. THE ROOM: the livery stable of a gold-rush town, 1850s, at night. Weathered plank walls, packed earth and straw on the floor, a hay loft above; well kept, everything hung in order.
- Far left: big double stable doors, wide enough for a coach, opened only to a narrow gap showing the dark street.
- Left: a tack wall of harness, bridles and collars hung by size, one collar enormous; in front of it on the floor, a blacksmith's anvil on a stump.
- Top, across the room: the hay loft, hay spilling over its edge; a ladder up to it, its rungs worn bright.
- Left of middle: a low open pen holding FOUR ordinary brown mules, side by side, standing side-on and turned toward the right, looking across at the stall in the middle.
- Middle: a post with a paper board of names pinned to it; against the wall beside it, a kitchen chair tipped back on two legs with the livery man in it.
- Right of middle: a stall with a half door, a name burned into its post, and the mule GRIEVANCE in it, looking out over the half door; a lantern hangs on the post beside it. Beside her stall a wooden feed bin with a striped wool blanket folded neatly on its lid.
- Right: more stalls, empty of animals: one with hay, one with a broken one-seat sulky, one stacked with folding chairs.
- Light: the lantern by Grievance's stall, warm; the corners darker.

THE PEOPLE AND ANIMALS (this picture is a reference painting, so they are allowed in it):
- The hero from image 4, standing where image 1 puts him, at exactly that size, facing right.
- THE LIVERY MAN, tipped back in his chair against the wall, facing right toward Grievance's stall: an old, lean stableman, weathered face, grey stubble, a battered hat, a faded work shirt with the sleeves rolled, braces, old boots; at ease, unhurried, a man who has seen everything once.
- GRIEVANCE, a mule the colour of a wet saddle, dark brown, long ears, looking out over her half door with an expression of settled disapproval.
- The four other mules: ordinary, contented, a little lighter brown.

THE LOOK: the same pixel-art style as images 2, 3 and 6, and the same hand as the hero: the SAME BIG SQUARE PIXELS as the hero -- every person and animal drawn on exactly the hero's coarse pixel grid, no finer -- flat, soft shading, very little texture, simple shapes. Warm browns and straw golds, leather, lantern light. Nothing near black where the hero stands.

RULES: no readable text, letters or numbers anywhere -- the board of names and the name on the post carry marks, never words. No border, no frame around the picture, no caption.
