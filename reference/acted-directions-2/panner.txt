Image 1 is a TEMPLATE on magenta from my pixel-art adventure game, set in an 1850s gold camp. The FIRST figure is the hero, Thad: he is THE STANDARD for the style, and is shown at his true size. The other figures are the river's panner, a rich man standing in cold water all day with a gold pan, in five poses and then once more. Image 2 is him beside Thad at game size. THE PROBLEM: the panner looks like Thad in a hat -- the same young face and build, the same long dark coat over a brown waistcoat, the same white collar.

Repaint Image 1. LEAVE THE FIRST FIGURE (THAD) EXACTLY AS IT IS. Repaint the other six as ONE DIFFERENT MAN who cannot be mistaken for Thad: older, past fifty, heavier in the face, with a full grey beard; a good, expensive coat of brown checked tweed, a dark green waistcoat with a gold watch chain, a red neckerchief, a bowler hat; dark trousers and boots. The same height as now, his feet on the same line. The same gold pan in his hands in every pose:
2. (REDRAWN) panning: the pan held low in both hands in front of him;
3. (REDRAWN) the swirl: the pan tilted, swirled in both hands;
4. (REDRAWN) looking upstream, to the right of the picture, the pan held at his side;
5. (REDRAWN) one hand closed tight on something small, held to his chest, the pan in the other hand;
6. (REDRAWN) talking, one hand raised a little, the pan in the other hand;
7. NEW POSE: he snorts -- his head thrown back a little, his mouth shut, a short scornful huff, the pan held low in both hands.
Nothing else is added to his hands.

The same size of square pixel, the same soft, flat shading, low texture and dark outline as THAD -- NOT sharper, NOT more detailed. Every figure the same height as the figure it repaints, its feet on the same line. Flat pure magenta (#FF00FF) background, every figure separated by clear magenta, nothing touching. No shadows, no ground, no furniture, no text, no numbers, no border.
