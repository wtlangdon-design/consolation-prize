Image 1 is a canvas on magenta from my pixel-art adventure game, set in an 1850s gold-rush town. The figure on the left is the hero, Thad, standing: he is THE STANDARD for the whole cast -- his flat, soft shading, his low texture, his proportions -- and he shows the size of a standing man. Image 2 is Thad in the saloon beside the bar and its drinkers: the new man stands BEHIND that bar counter, in front of the shelves of bottles, and this is how flat, soft and low in texture the game's drawing is. Image 3 is one of the drinkers at that bar, drawn to the same standard: match him.

On the magenta to the right of Thad, draw FOUR full-length standing figures of ONE new man, in a row, each separated by clear magenta, the same height as Thad, their feet on the same line as his: the saloon's barman. A heavy-set, unhurried man of about fifty, a neat moustache, shirt sleeves rolled up and held by garters on his upper arms, a dark waistcoat, a long white apron tied at the waist, dark trousers, a clean white towel over one shoulder. He faces us, three-quarters toward the left. His hands work in front of him at the height of his belt (that is where the bar counter will be).
1. polishing a small glass with the towel, the towel's end in one hand, the glass in the other;
2. the same, the glass turned and the towel moved;
3. leaning a little forward, setting the glass down at the height of his belt, the glass under his hand; the towel back over his shoulder;
4. holding the glass against his chest, his head turned to look along the bar to his left, the towel over his shoulder.
The towel is on him in every figure. The same small glass in every figure.

The same size of square pixel, the same soft, flat shading, low texture and dark outline as THAD -- NOT sharper, NOT more detailed. Flat pure magenta (#FF00FF) background, nothing touching. No bar, no counter, no floor, no shadows, no text, no numbers, no border.
