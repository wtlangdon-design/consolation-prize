Paint this place as a finished game background with both men in it, keeping the camera, the layout and every block where image 1 puts it. THE PLACE: the gold camp at the bottom of a steep mountain ravine in the frontier West, 1850s, on a clear DAY. It is the camp seen from high above in image 2, now seen from the ground among its tents. A camp that has found real gold and is nervous about it. No town buildings, no false fronts, no boardwalk: only canvas, logs and trodden earth, hemmed in by the ravine walls. Daylight, a pale blue strip of sky high up between the walls.
- The ground where the hero stands: bare trodden earth, a middle brown, readable, not dark and not pale.
- At the far LEFT: the bank going down out of the picture to a river; a glimpse of fast water at the very bottom left.
- Back left: two small log cabins with chimneys of sticks and mud. Their plank doors are shut, each with several iron bolts.
- Back, left of centre: a big canvas tent with its front flap tied back, a dark doorway, and a plain wooden board over the doorway with NO writing on it.
- Back, centre right: two canvas tents, each pegged down twice over and held with many guy ropes, like a ship; on one tent a small plain board hangs with NO writing on it.
- Between those two tents: a washing line with a few store-bought shirts hanging on it, each shirt with a small padlock on its pocket.
- Middle, in front of the tents: a cook fire of stones with a kettle hanging over it and a frying pan on the stones, a thin line of smoke going straight up.
- Right of centre: a tree stump with a plain pale paper notice nailed to it, with NO writing on it.
- At the RIGHT: the steep rocky ravine wall going up, with a narrow path zigzagging up it and a rope strung along the worst parts on wooden pegs, the same path as in image 2.
- ALL THE CANVAS IS WEATHERED, DUSTY, TAN-GREY: a middle tone like the ground, NOT bright white.
