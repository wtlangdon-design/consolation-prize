# PART ONE — HOW TO RUN THIS

## The consistency method

Text prompts alone will drift. The reliable technique is an anchor image:

1. **Generate Main Street first.** Iterate until it is right. This is the hardest and most-seen room in the game, and everything else is judged against it.
2. **Use it as a visual reference on every subsequent generation.** Most tools accept a reference image; use it every time, without exception.
3. **Generate the other five hero rooms next**, referencing Main Street. Confirm they read as one town.
4. **Only then generate the remaining 35.**

## The style block is not optional

**Paste the master style block verbatim at the top of every single prompt.** Do not paraphrase it, do not shorten it, do not "improve" it between rooms. Consistency comes from it being identical 41 times.

## Adventure-game technical requirements

These matter more than beauty. A gorgeous painting with no floor is unusable.

- **No people. No characters. No figures. No animals.** Everything alive is a sprite drawn on top. A background with a painted-in person is unusable.
- **No text, lettering, or signage copy.** Image models mangle text. All signs must be blank, weathered illegible, or angled away. Lettering gets added in post.
- **Camera at standing eye level — roughly five and a half feet — perfectly horizontal, no tilt, no wide-angle distortion.** A character sprite has to stand in this scene and not look wrong.
- **Clear, uncluttered open ground across the lower third of the frame.** This is the walkable area. Objects belong at the sides and in the middle distance.
- **Interactive objects must read as separate silhouettes**, not blended into texture. If a hotspot is a pickaxe, the pickaxe needs its own shape against its background.
- **16:9, 1920×1080.**
- **Light from frame left in every exterior. Interiors lit by a window at frame left.** One sun, one town.

# PART THREE — THE SIX HERO ROOMS

*Generate these first, in this order. Full prompts.*

## 1 · MAIN STREET — the anchor

> [MASTER STYLE BLOCK]
>
> A wide frontier main street, seen straight down its length. Wooden buildings on both sides with tall false fronts — each façade two storeys tall but the building behind it visibly only one storey deep, the deception readable from this angle. Raised wooden boardwalks on both sides, six inches above deep churned mud in the road. A water trough. A hitching rail. Blank weathered notice boards. One grander building on the left with fresh, expensive paint, conspicuously better maintained than its neighbours. Bare brown hills closing the far end of the street. A church steeple in the distance, the only building as deep as it is tall. Everything else pretending very hard.

## 2 · THE BOUNTIFUL NUGGET — saloon interior

> [MASTER STYLE BLOCK — amend to: interior, lit by a large window at frame left and oil lamps]
>
> The interior of a frontier saloon. A long polished mahogany bar along the right — genuinely expensive, the only quality surface in the room. A dirt floor. An ornate brass and crystal chandelier hanging over the dirt floor, absurdly out of place. An upright piano against the left wall, scratched from a long overland journey. Rough tables and chairs. A staircase at the back. A blank handbill pinned to the wall by the door. Warm dusty light in shafts from the window. Open floor space in the foreground.

## 3 · PROSPERITY — the claim

> [MASTER STYLE BLOCK]
>
> A small, worthless mining claim on a bare hillside. Roughly forty feet of churned mud and grey rock, marked at the corners by rough wooden stakes. A dark irregular hole in the rock face at centre-right, roughly waist-high, going back into the hillside. A rusted pan and a broken shovel handle. Sparse dry scrub. A collapsed lean-to. Bare hills receding. Utterly unremarkable ground. The composition should make the viewer wonder why anyone would want it.

## 4 · THE ASSAY OFFICE — front room

> [MASTER STYLE BLOCK — amend to: interior, lit by a window at frame left]
>
> A small, meticulously organised frontier assay office. A wooden counter across the middle distance with a barred service window. Behind it, shelves of small labelled sample vials in perfect order. A precision balance scale under a glass dome, immaculate. A cast iron stove. Ledger books stacked square. A blank handwritten card pinned by the window. Plain plank floor, swept clean — the tidiest room in the territory. Everything squared away, nothing decorative, one competent person's domain. Open floor in the foreground.

## 5 · FANSHAWE'S OFFICE

> [MASTER STYLE BLOCK — amend to: interior, lit by a tall window at frame left]
>
> A richly appointed frontier businessman's office, far grander than the town outside it. A large polished desk. Eleven leather armchairs arranged around the room — far too many chairs for the space. A cavalry sabre mounted on the wall, ornamental and unused. A large framed portrait in an ornate gilt frame, the face turned away from the viewer or in shadow. A wall of pinned survey maps. A crystal decanter, full, on a side table. A Turkish rug. Warm, generous, comfortable, expensive — and completely airless.

## 6 · BOOT HILL

> [MASTER STYLE BLOCK]
>
> A small frontier cemetery on a dusty rise above a town. Perhaps two dozen weathered wooden grave markers at slightly wrong angles, all blank. One marker noticeably better made and better maintained than the rest, near the centre, with dried flowers at its base. One freshly dug open grave in the foreground with a mound of earth beside it. Dry grass, low scrub, a leaning fence. The town visible below in the middle distance, small and false-fronted. Long low light.

# PART FIVE — QC

Before accepting any background, check:

1. **No figures.** Any painted-in person makes it unusable.
2. **No readable text.** Signage must be blank or illegible.
3. **Walkable ground** across the lower third, unobstructed.
4. **Camera height matches Main Street.** Hold them side by side; the horizon should sit at the same height.
5. **Light from frame left.**
6. **Palette matches.** Any background that introduces a new dominant colour is out.

**The test that matters:** put Main Street and Main Street At Dawn side by side and ask whether they are the same street. If they aren't, the dawn ending doesn't work — and that shot is carrying the emotional close of the entire game.
