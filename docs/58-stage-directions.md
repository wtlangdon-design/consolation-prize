# THE LAST CLAIM IN CONSOLATION
## 58 · Stage directions on screen — which are performed, which are notes

*Tyler, 2026-11-05: "Proceed." The script audit found that over half of all conversation replies in docs 04 and 27 carry an italic stage direction — `*(He hands Thad the pen. The pen has no ink.)*` — and that the extractor dropped every one. What reached the screen was the clipped line with its setup cut away: "Raccoon." "All right." "He's in there." without the reach into the hole and the bite that "Healing wrong." pays off for the rest of the game. Half the comedy of the trees lived in those brackets.*

*From this document on, a direction in a conversation reply is **shown**: in the order the document writes it, over the character, in the quieter ink the game already uses for a beat, in parentheses. Nothing is invented and nothing is retyped — the extractor reads the words from docs 04 and 27 exactly as written.*

---

## The rules

1. **Shown by default.** A direction inside a reply, before the `→`, is performed — its text appears in its place in the exchange.
2. **A bare pause is an ellipsis.** `*(beat)*`, `*(pause)*` and `*(long pause)*` are shown as the character saying "…". Any other wording of a pause ("a long, respectful pause", "after a very long pause", "a long pause, the first in his life") is shown as written, because the wording is the joke.
3. **Notes are never shown.** A direction is a note — for the builder, not the player — when it carries any of: `errata`, `req `, `sets `, `doc ` + a number, `pair`, `RULED`, `Superseded`, `Tyler,`, `→`, `the player`, or a selection marker (`second`, `third` …). The extractor hides these by pattern, so a new note in a new room is hidden without anyone listing it.
4. **And the ones listed below are hidden by name.** Each is a note in plain words, or would give away a puzzle or the mystery if the player read it.
5. **A direction is only interleaved where the extractor's own reading of the spoken lines agrees with the document.** Where a tree's extractor reads a reply specially (Hob's "— Thad:" asides, a rephrase, a duel's opening), the reply is left as it was and reported, never re-parsed into something different.

## Hidden by name

- Available only when it has not been.
- Every time. It is never acknowledged.
- The first assay the player ever watches happen.
- Not an ending.
- True. Also the most complete lie in the game.
- This is the puzzle. He is being paid two dollars a week to acquire something being offered eleven thousand.
- Every time, for the rest of the game, in every act, including the ending.
- She has been reading ground resonance for eleven years and has never once considered that she is listening.
- He is exactly right, one ridge to the left.
- It is a stock portrait. Sheng has the same one in his window, advertising collars.
- This is the complete answer, and the only eulogy Pike gets until Thad reads the headboard.
- Load-bearing for the funeral theme and the man does not know it.
- +4 adjectives, $8
- +0
- +0, free, unprompted.
- second, softer
- third, to himself, genuinely moved
- If unresolved:
- This is true, is never followed up, and is not needed for anything.
- The swindle. Four dollars and the watch for the deed.

*Why these: the first eight explain the design or what the player is meant to notice; the next three (Ozymandia's resonance, Sheng's ridge, the stock portrait) would hand over the mystery's answers in narration that the player is supposed to reach by listening and looking; the counters are Sowerby's price mechanic, which is not text; the next three are layout notes; the last names the puzzle the row completes.*
