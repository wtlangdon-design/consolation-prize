# PART ONE — HOW MONKEY ISLAND SOUNDS, AND HOW WE GET THERE

The first voice pass (docs 60–93) polished lines and was not allowed to change what anybody said or wanted. That made the writing smoother and left it timid. These are the things Monkey Island does that our script mostly does not.

### 1 · Everyone is fully committed to their own bit
Nobody in Monkey Island is in on the joke. The pirate leaders really do just want grog, and the salesman really does think he's helping. **No character winks, apologises for, or explains their absurdity.** Each believes their own premise completely and argues for it. Comedy comes from two people who are each completely sure of themselves, about opposite things.

> Ours before: the pie woman deflects ("Pie's a nickel.").
> Ours after: the pie woman *sells* — she prices him on sight and answers every question in money.

### 2 · Thad says the dumb thing out loud, and means it
Guybrush brags, overshares, misreads the room and charges on. **Thad is eager, sincere, a little vain about his ear and very interested in gold, and he says so.** He takes every hint too literally and every compliment too seriously. He is never sarcastic, and never the cleverest person in the scene unless the scene is about pitch.

### 3 · The option list is a joke menu
Half the comedy in Monkey Island is reading the four things the hero could say. **Every node's options should be worth reading before one is clicked:**
- one plain question that moves things on;
- one that is too familiar, too greedy or too honest;
- one that is plainly the wrong thing to say, which a player picks *because* it is wrong (the `[COMIC]` option);
- an exit that is itself a joke (doc 04's pool, now rotating).

An option that is just a polite question ("What do you do here?") is a wasted line. Make it the question Thad would actually ask, which is usually about money.

### 4 · Answers escalate; they don't deflate
A reply that shuts the player down ("No." "Penny." "Tomorrow.") lands once. Monkey Island's characters **build on whatever Thad says**: they take the premise further, add a detail nobody asked for, or get offended about the wrong part. A shut-down answer is allowed once per character, as their signature, and never twice in a row.

### 5 · Information arrives inside a joke, and always points somewhere
Every conversation the player is likely to have early should leave them **knowing where to go next**, without anyone saying "you should go to X". The Pike thread on Main Street is the model: the town tells him the way while refusing to tell him the truth.

### 6 · The institutions are modern, and nobody finds it odd
Doc 01's anachronism pillar is our equivalent of Monkey Island's grog machines and used-ship lot. **Forms, queues, take-a-number spindles, customer service and quarterly results**, delivered by people in waistcoats as if that were normal. Use it hardest on Sump, Fanshawe, the hotel clerk and the Company. Never in technology.

### 7 · Rhythm: set up, beat, punch — and short
A line is read once, over a head, at a click. **Setup and punch in one sentence each, at most two sentences a turn,** and a beat (`*(beat)*` or Thad's one-line interjection) before a punch that needs one. If a joke needs three sentences, split it across the exchange so Thad gets a line in the middle.

### 8 · Running gags are the spine
They are what make a world feel authored. Ours, to run and escalate across the whole game:
- **The town's spellings of his name** (Frame, the register, the funeral notice);
- **"Course you will."** — the pie woman's, and only hers;
- **Four dollars** — what Thad has, until Vessel has it;
- **The flat C-sharp, and Thad's ear** — he hears what is wrong with everything; nobody else can;
- **Fanshawe's war** — its details never agree with each other;
- **The man leaving tomorrow**, whose tomorrow moves;
- **The bear in the Nugget's corner story**, which keeps getting bigger;
- **Ezra Pike**, whom everybody knows about and nobody will mention.

A new gag may be grown in a room if it carries no information (invariant 9's spirit).

### 9 · Warmth
Monkey Island's world likes its hero, however badly he does. **Consolation is ridiculous, never cruel.** People rob Thad cheerfully and are sorry when he dies. Nobody is mean except Fanshawe, and he is *nice* about it.

### 10 · What we never do
- Reuse a Monkey Island line, gag or name. We take the *shape*, never the words.
- Explain a joke, or have a character laugh at their own.
- Use a pop-culture reference, a modern word in a person's mouth ("okay", "awesome"), or a wink at the player that breaks the world. Monkey Island's fourth-wall jokes are a spice we are not using.
- Mark a load-bearing LISTEN line, or let a `[COMIC]` option do anything (invariants 1, 3).

# PART TWO — THE CAST

Each entry gives five things:
- **Wants**: what they are after in every scene.
- **Of Thad**: what they think of him.
- **Talks**: their sound.
- **Runs**: their gag.
- **Never**: the line they do not cross.

The facts in each come from docs 01, 04, 07 and 27. The angles are new.

## THADDEUS GRUBB
- **Wants:** gold, in quantity, soon. Second, to be taken seriously as a man with an ear.
- **Of himself:** a man of business who happens to be a piano tuner, on the brink of a fortune.
- **Talks:** contractions; quick; enthusiastic. Prices things without being asked. Reports disaster calmly and hope excitedly. Says "I have decided" once a room, when the formality is the joke.
- **Runs:**
  - his ear: he can hear what is wrong with anything and is a little proud of it;
  - his four dollars;
  - his father's letter, which he carries like a passport.
- **Never:** sarcastic, weary, cruel, or the cleverest person in a scene that isn't about sound.

## MISS WINNIE LEDGER — assayer
- **Wants:** to be left to her work. Underneath, nine years in, to be right about Mott.
- **Of Thad:** the first interesting thing in nine years, which she finds irritating.
- **Talks:** as few words as the sentence allows; numbers; corrections. Every answer is a ruling.
- **Runs:** her queue (nine hundred men ahead of him); her window closing at four; "Get in the book."
- **Never:** softens anything, explains herself twice, or flirts. When she shows feeling, it's one word too many and she notices.

## COLONEL ABSALOM FANSHAWE — the Improvement Company
- **Wants:** the search to go on forever, because the search is his business.
- **Of Thad:** a delightful young man and an asset to be managed. He likes him, truly. It changes nothing.
- **Talks:** expansive, warm, courtly; never refuses; speaks in a promoter's language of opportunity and partnership. Every gift comes with a cost so polite you don't notice it.
- **Runs:** the war, whose details never match; "Very wise"; the handkerchief.
- **Never:** raises his voice, says no, or admits anything. A threat from him sounds like an invitation to dinner.

## HOB — night watchman, and Obadiah Mott
- **Wants:** to be overlooked.
- **Of Thad:** a man who listens, which is the most dangerous kind.
- **Talks:** forty words across Acts I–II, in total. Answers a different question from the one he's asked, correctly.
- **Runs:** "No."; walking on; being exactly where nobody looks.
- **Never:** jokes, explains, or says a word that isn't true. Every line is a clue read backwards; none can be marked (invariants 3, 7).

## IGNATIUS FRAME — the *Clarion*
- **Wants:** a story by the next edition, true or not, and ideally about Thad.
- **Of Thad:** the best thing that has happened to circulation since Mott.
- **Talks:** mid-sentence, headline-shaped, CAPITALS when he's composing. Asks for a quote about anything.
- **Runs:** Thad's name spelled wrong, differently each time; the *Clarion* printing Thad's humiliations within the hour; "I wrote most of it."
- **Never:** malicious. He can't tell a fact from a good line, and doesn't see why he should.

## NICKEL PRATT — claim jumper
- **Wants:** Prosperity. He couldn't tell you why. He's being paid.
- **Of Thad:** in the way, then in trouble, then, surprisingly, his.
- **Talks:** slow, one idea at a time, numbers. He's unhurried, not stupid.
- **Runs:** a new offer at a new number; the raccoon bite ("Healing wrong.").
- **Never:** clever, quick, or cruel. His one brave moment is played straight.

## MADAME OZYMANDIA — diviner
- **Wants:** the fee.
- **Of Thad:** a customer, then, uncomfortably, a colleague.
- **Talks:** enormous theatrical authority, mystic grammar ("The rod *seeks*…"), dropped instantly at the mention of money and resumed mid-word.
- **Runs:** charging for everything, including the comic options; the humming.
- **Never:** admits fraud, or notices that her method works.

## AH-LAM SHENG — laundry, and the town's only bank
- **Wants:** his accounts in order, and a quiet ridge.
- **Of Thad:** genuinely funny. The only person in town who thinks so, and he shows it by correcting Thad's arithmetic.
- **Talks:** precise, dry, economical. The straight man the town doesn't deserve.
- **Runs:** knowing exactly what everyone owes; the Reverend's linen.
- **Never:** the butt of a joke. Every joke in his scenes is at the town's expense. Full dignity always.

## REVEREND SOWERBY
- **Wants:** a funeral, any funeral, paid for.
- **Of Thad:** a bereaved person, potentially. Later, a client, and then a corpse.
- **Talks:** pulpit cadence, adjectives by the yard, sudden grief at full volume for strangers. He means every word, every time, at his rates.
- **Runs:** charging by the adjective; "I KNOW."; the anniversary service.
- **Never:** a hypocrite. He really feels it, all of it, for money.

## REGISTRAR CADWALLADER SUMP
- **Wants:** the statute followed, in order.
- **Of Thad:** an applicant.
- **Talks:** citations: form numbers, section numbers, "in that order". Completely helpful, completely immovable.
- **Runs:** a form for everything (12-F: Requests, Facetious); the take-a-number spindle.
- **Never:** corrupt, or moved. The one man in Consolation the paperwork protects.

## DEKE VESSEL — the Nugget
- **Wants:** Thad's four dollars and his watch, and to be out of Consolation by morning.
- **Of Thad:** a man who wants to be sold to, which is the best kind.
- **Talks:** sunny, chummy, delighted with himself; leans in; calls Thad "friend".
- **Runs:** "I'm getting out."; the producing claim.
- **Never:** says anything untrue. Read back afterwards, every word was technically accurate.

## THE UNDERTAKER
- **Wants:** to be paid, eventually, by somebody.
- **Of Thad:** a future client, with no hurry about it.
- **Talks:** fewest words possible, then waits. Dates and plot numbers. Not unkind.
- **Runs:** "Eighteen fifty-two."; who owes him (Pike does).
- **Never:** theatrical. He is the one person in town who tells Thad the truth plainly, which is why everyone else sends him there.

## THE STAGE DRIVER
- **Wants:** off this road, and out of this town, today.
- **Of Thad:** freight that talks.
- **Talks:** short, put-upon, always halfway back up on the box.
- **Runs:** the dry axle he won't fix; "Ask the undertaker."
- **Never:** helps beyond one sentence.

## THE LIVERY MAN
- **Wants:** Grievance happy, which means nobody touching anything.
- **Of Thad:** a man Grievance has noticed, which is bad news for both of them.
- **Talks:** speaks for the mule. "She doesn't like that." "She's thinking."
- **Runs:** consulting Grievance before every answer.
- **Never:** overrules the mule.

## THE HOTEL CLERK
- **Wants:** the register kept, and spelled his way.
- **Of Thad:** a guest, and therefore a problem.
- **Talks:** proprietorial, procedural, "as given". Hotel policy for everything.
- **Runs:** his spelling of Thad's name, which disagrees with Frame's and is authoritative.
- **Never:** rude. He is administering.

## THE TOWNSPEOPLE (doc 07: one joke each, run forever)

The ambient rule stands: each is **one premise**, fully committed, reacting to Thad's reputation and never to the plot. The change is that each has a **point of view on Thad** and an **angle on whatever he asks**, so the one joke can answer anything.

- **The letter-writer.** *Wants:* custom. *Angle:* every life is material, and the truth is a cheap edition. Charmed by Thad's honesty the way you're charmed by a child's drawing.
- **The boundary men.** *Want:* the argument, forever. *Angle:* anything Thad says becomes evidence for one side. They agree only against outsiders.
- **The message boy.** *Wants:* pennies. *Angle:* knowledge is a market and he's cornered it. Nine years old and the shrewdest businessman in town.
- **The pie woman.** *Wants:* a sale. *Angle:* every question is a negotiation. She prices you on sight. "Course you will" is hers alone.
- **The man leaving tomorrow.** *Wants:* to leave, tomorrow. *Angle:* every topic is about leaving; Thad arriving offends him slightly.
- **The map seller.** *Wants:* to share the joy of Mott's strike. *Angle:* the true believer. Every fact proves the maps; Thad's land on the map is the best news of his life.
- **The bell-ringer.** *Wants:* to be left drunk. *Angle:* the bell is loud, which is its job. Accuracy is someone else's department.
- **The fiddler.** *Wants:* to stay tuned to the piano. *Angle:* agreement over accuracy. Being right alone would be unbearable.
- **The one-strike man.** *Wants:* to tell it again. *Angle:* one strike, in '49, on a Tuesday, and every conversation leads back to it.
- **The card sharp.** *Wants:* a game. *Angle:* he cheats openly and is the only honest man at the table about it. Likes Thad too much to take his money.
- **The dog's owner.** *Wants:* Bill. *Angle:* every conversation is about the dog.
- **The Nugget's stove man and landing man; the duel judges; the sign-painter.** Painted or staged figures. Each gets one consistent attitude when they speak:
  - the stove man: cold, in July;
  - the landing man: waiting;
  - the judges: one pompous, one pedantic, one bored;
  - the sign-painter: never finished.

## THE ANIMALS
- **Grievance** and **the raccoon** never speak. Every line about them is Thad's. Grievance has an opinion of him. The raccoon has a full Christian name and no regrets.
