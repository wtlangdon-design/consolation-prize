# THE LAST CLAIM IN CONSOLATION
## Puzzle Dependency Graph — v1

*Companion to Bible v2. 45 puzzles (the table below is the count of record) · 4 acts · ~40 items · no dead ends.*

---

## Graph rules

1. **Nothing is destroyable.** No item can be permanently consumed in a way that blocks a later solution. Where an item is "spent," a replacement path exists.
2. **The three trials of Act II are fully independent.** Any order, any interleaving. A player stuck on one always has two others open.
3. **No timed failures.** Nothing expires. The Registrar's four o'clock window is a puzzle, not a clock.
4. **Every act reuses at least two items or topics from a previous act.** This is the Monkey Island signature and the thing most likely to be lost in implementation.
5. **Every puzzle chain is at least three steps.** No fetch quests. If a puzzle can be stated as "bring X to Y," it needs another layer.

# ACT II — *Three Pieces of Paper*

**The objective:** a **Certificate of Contiguity** (Document C), the only instrument that permits a neighbouring claimholder to view Claim 1's sealed original filing.

Document C requires **A**, **B**, and **D**. Three independent trials. Any order.

## Dead-end audit

- **Money:** $4 is spent in A3 and never required again. The filing fee is earned in A8 by a repeatable action — Thad can tune the Nugget piano as many times as needed, and the joke improves with repetition.
- **Trials:** A, B and D have no shared prerequisites. Failing to progress one never blocks the others. B6 requires 14 of the ~24 available pairs, and 14 are obtainable from Trials Two and Three alone.
- **The funeral:** C3 and D4 both need a funeral day. One funeral satisfies both, and further funerals can be arranged — Sowerby is always available and always expensive.
- **Pratt:** hostile from A5 onward but never blocking. Every Pratt interaction has a non-Pratt alternative until D5, where turning him is the puzzle.
- **The coffin (E7):** the one sequence with no verb panel. It is not a fail state and cannot be lost — it resolves on a timer. This is the deliberate, signposted exception to the "you always have control" rule, and it is exactly three minutes.
- **F2:** the only puzzle in the game with no text hint. Mitigation: the score's tuning provides continuous non-verbal feedback, and the tap resonance is unambiguous once the player stops looking at the screen.
