## B2 · Why Grievance moves: from two questions to two things noticed

**Today:** two questions to the livery man in a row. "Has she ever moved for anybody?" tells the story of the man who whistled her out twelve years ago (`T_GRIEVANCE_MOTT`). Then "What's that tune?" gets the tune he has been humming wrong ever since (`T_HUM_HEARD`). Then the fork on Grievance (B3).

**Proposed:** the player finds each thing out by looking and listening. The livery man answers what they have noticed.

**1 · The board.** THE BOARD OF NAMES already says, on its first LOOK: *"Who has hired what, going back years. Twelve years ago somebody hired Grievance for four days and did not write down where they were going."* Reading it opens the question. Unchanged line; it now writes `T_BOARD_GRIEVANCE`, unmarked.

The livery man's option 6 is asked from what the board says, and shown only once it has been read:

> **THAD:** "Your board says she went out once. Twelve years ago, four days, and nobody wrote down where."
> **LIVERY MAN:** "Once. Twelve years back. Fella came in at night, whistled at her, and she walked out after him like she'd been waiting." *(his line, unchanged)*
> **LIVERY MAN:** "Never saw him again. Never saw her move again, either." *(unchanged)*
> *(He tips his chair back and goes on humming, under his breath.)* **← new direction**

**2 · The hum.** LISTEN TO THE LIVERY MAN. New, three lines each way, as every LISTEN in the game has:

*Before he has told the story:*
> 1 "Breathing, and the chair. He is a man at rest, and he means to stay one."
> 2 "Nothing. He is listening to me listen to him."
> 3 "The chair creaks. He has tipped it back another degree, to make a point."

*After (the first writes `T_HUM_HEARD`, unmarked):*
> 1 "He's humming. The same eight bars, under his breath, over and over. The fourth bar sags every time, like a step worn hollow."
> 2 "The tune again. The fourth bar sags again. Twelve years of practice have made it very reliably wrong."
> 3 "Still humming. It is the only wrong note in the building, and the building is mostly wrong notes."

Then "What's that tune?" is asked from what was heard (shown once `T_HUM_HEARD` is set), and his answer is unchanged: *"That's what the fella whistled. Twelve years I've been getting it wrong."*

**What stays:** B3 itself, every line of it; the flags B3 and HOB_C2 read (`T_GRIEVANCE_MOTT`, `T_HUM_HEARD`); the three load-bearing LISTEN lines, untouched; no hint, no marking; nobody can get stuck, because the board and the livery man are in the same room and both are always there.

**Why it is better:** B2 becomes the game's own idea, which is a piano tuner who notices a wrong note. The player hears the sagging bar before anyone explains it, so the fork on the mule (B3) is their own deduction rather than the end of a conversation.
