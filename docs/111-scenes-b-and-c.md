## Why B moved

Doc 110 had the séance replacing E5.2, Frame writing the obituary at his desk (FRA_E1). Read closely, FRA_E1 is one of the game's best quiet scenes:

- Frame declines a pun for the only time in his life;
- "he was the only man in town who fixed anything";
- the correctly spelled name goes, on a separate slip, to the stonecutter. That is errata 9's payoff on the grave marker.

It is not paperwork. It stays exactly as written.

The séance takes E1's place instead. E1 is Thad's attempt to have Obadiah Mott declared dead, which fails by design. Today that attempt is a refusal across Sump's counter. As a séance it becomes a scene, and it brings Ozymandia back into Act III.

## B · The séance for Obadiah Mott (replaces E1's petition)

### What the game already gives it

- Ozymandia's fees: "Two dollars a week — in advance —". Her brass rod, which isn't drawn to gold. She has been reading ground resonance for eleven years without considering that she is listening.
- Frame, who prints anything quotable.
- The town's legend of Mott, mostly Frame's invention.
- **Mott is alive.** He is Hob (E9, E10). So a séance for him can only fail. Played straight, that is the joke. On a replay it is a seed, planted deniably, like Hob's "Better than mine." Nothing marks it.

### The situation

The evening after Fanshawe's offer (E0b), Thad has decided to read Claim One's sealed filing. The executor of a dead claimholder may, and Mott has been missing twelve years. Ozymandia has a sign out in the Lane: a séance tonight for Obadiah Mott, if available, at two dollars a seat. The town has paid. Frame has a front-row seat and a pencil.

### The puzzle

1. **Getting in.** Thad has no two dollars.
   - The table is full, and Ozymandia won't seat a man who hasn't paid.
   - LOOK at the table: the cloth reaches the floor.
2. **Getting under.** The lamp is lit, and the town would see him.
   - Thad asks Ozymandia whether the dead mind the light. They do, she has always said so, and she puts it out.
   - In the dark, USE THE TABLE: Thad is under the cloth.
3. **The séance.** Ozymandia calls on the spirit of Obadiah Mott, and nothing comes.
   - She tries again, and again nothing comes.
   - She consults the rod: "The spirits say he is… busy."
4. **The voice from beneath.** A short dialogue, Thad speaking as Mott's ghost to the table:
   - several [COMIC] options;
   - one that does the job: a dead man saying, plainly, that he is dead.
   - Frame has it in print before the lamp is lit again.
5. **The next morning at the Registrar's.** Thad presents the Clarion's front page to Sump.
   - Sump doesn't accept testimony from the deceased. The office has tried.
   - **E1** is complete, blocked by design, as now.
   - Section nineteen (E2) follows in the same visit, as now. SUM_E1 option 1 stays for a player who goes straight to Sump.

### Wrong tries

To be written, one line each:

- PULL the tablecloth;

- sitting down without paying;
- each COMIC "ghost" option, with the table's reaction.

### What it needs

- **Art:** Ozymandia's tent at night, set for a séance: the round table with its floor-length cloth, the lamp, and four or five of the town seated, Frame among them. That is one companion state of her tent plus a seated group, from `act3-seance` (four operations).
- **Sound:** a candle-lit hush, and one table knock. Auditioned for Tyler, as the room beds were.
- **Puzzles:** the count is unchanged. The séance becomes E1's way of completing.

## C · The estate sale (replaces Sheng's loan, E3.2)

### What the game already gives it

- **Pratt's offer.** Every conversation ends in it, and it never stops: "Nine dollars." · "Nine dollars and a hen." · "Eleven dollars, and I'll take the raccoon." · "Fourteen dollars, and you keep the raccoon." · …
- **The raccoon.** Pratt has been bitten by him (A5), and the bite is a standing topic. He has put the raccoon in and out of his offer all game.
- **The undertaker's bill** is exactly two dollars and sixty cents. Sheng knows it before Thad does, which is the joke of E3.2 today.

### The situation

Thad needs two dollars and sixty cents to pay for his own funeral. He has no money. Pratt has always wanted the raccoon, or not wanted it, depending on the offer. The raccoon has come back to Prosperity's hole, as he regards it as his.

### The puzzle

1. **The offer, turned round.** Thad offers Pratt "the effects of the late Mr. Grubb", in advance.
   - Pratt isn't interested in effects. He is interested in the raccoon. He doesn't fetch.
   - His price is two dollars and sixty cents. He asked the undertaker what Thad would need.
2. **The raccoon won't come out.**
   - A hand in the hole is the same mistake Pratt made in Act I.
   - The raccoon has opinions, and the hole is warm.
3. **The bait.** Pratt's hen, on loan. The hen comes back; he is very clear about that.
   - The hen at the hole's mouth brings the raccoon halfway out.
   - Thad's coat over him does the rest. This is physical: a man in his shirtsleeves, wrestling a coat with a raccoon in it.
4. **The sale.** The raccoon, in the coat, to Pratt.
   - Pratt counts out two dollars and sixty cents, and takes back the hen.
   - He offers to let Thad keep the coat, which he regards as generous.
   - The money goes to the undertaker, exactly as Sheng's loan did (E3.3). The puzzle count is unchanged.
5. **A side effect that matters.** The hole is empty of raccoons for Act IV's dig.

### Sheng

Sheng's loan row stays in his tree only for a save already past it, as doc 110 §1 says. His Act II favour still pays off in D5.

### Wrong tries

To be written:
- a hand in the hole;
- the tuning fork on the raccoon;
- the coat on the hole without bait;
- the hen to the raccoon without the coat;
- the raccoon to the undertaker ("He doesn't take livestock");
- the raccoon to Sheng.

### What it needs

- **Art:** the raccoon in a coat, carried, and Pratt's hen.
  - The raccoon's sheet and Pratt's figures already exist.
  - That is up to two figures and an item icon or two, from `act3-estate-sale` (four operations).
- **Puzzles:** the count is unchanged. The sale is E3.2's new way of completing.
