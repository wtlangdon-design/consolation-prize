# 114 · The whole game, to its best: the plan (2026-11-09)

Tyler, 2026-11-09: "Merge and then make EVERYTHING in the game the best it can be. I don't want bandaids, I want perfection." Asked how, he chose **"Design first, then I rule"** for the writing and **"Design for the best scene, brief the art"** for the pictures.

So this document designs and rules nothing. It writes no line. **Each row of §4 is Tyler's to approve or strike.** An approved row gets its lines written (one voice, in /docs), its art briefed to the image session, and is built and played before the next.

It is written from the whole game played in the real build tonight (errata 241, PR 327), the transcript read end to end, and every frame looked at.

## 1 · What "best" means here, so it can be checked

"Until it's perfect" has no exit, and CLAUDE.md forbids working to it. These six do, and each can be held by a script over the played route:

| # | The standard | Today |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Every goal is won by doing, not only by asking.** Each puzzle the player would name has at least one step done with his hands | Fails for the neighbor's paper (D2–D6), the survey's two visits to Sump, and the archive |
| 2 | **Never more than three stops in a row where all he can do is choose a line** | Fails once: four in a row at the start of Act II. The back of Act II passes only narrowly after errata 242 |
| 3 | **Nothing the player sets in motion happens out of his sight** | Fails for Pike's memorial and the collection from the Reverend |
| 4 | **Every verb on the panel solves something somewhere, and an item is used on another item at least five times** *(five: Tyler, 2026-11-09, "Yes")* | Fails: PUSH, PULL and CLOSE solve nothing in the game, and no item is ever used on another item |
| 5 | **Each act has one scene a player would describe to a friend** | Act I has none of its own. Act II has the river. Act III has the hearse and the coffin. Act IV has the last duel |
| 6 | **No scene is reachable only by accident** | Fails for the stake (errata 242), and for Winnie's sparring lesson |

When all six hold on the played route, and Tyler has played it, this plan is done. That is the exit.

## 2 · What is already right, and stays

Not everything needs work, and saying so is part of the audit.

- **The opening**, to the mud.
- **The hotel piano** and the two dollars at the Nugget. Tuning the same piano twice is a running joke ("the right note was making him nervous"), not a repeat.
- **The swindle.** It is a conversation because a confidence trick is one.
- **The blanket and the raccoon.**
- **The whole road to Fiddler's Gulch and back**: the whistle, the lookout, the watch, the fiddle, the river, the relay of insults.
- **The séance, the estate sale, the hearse, the coffin, the confession.**
- **All of Act IV.** Its quiet is the design.
- Every invariant in CLAUDE.md. Nothing below touches a load-bearing line, the letters home, the hint-less rule or Room 32.

---

## 3 · What is not yet right, by act

**Act I** has good jokes and no scene of its own. Its biggest moment, the dinner with Fanshawe ("the table laid absurdly well"), is two lines chosen from a list and a handkerchief. The survey is a fee handed over and a map shown. And the played route walks to Pike's grave, the reason Thad came three thousand miles, and nothing is said on arriving.

**Act II, the three papers,** is better than it was this morning and is still the game's weakest hour. The neighbor's paper is errands. Pike's memorial is never seen. The two sparring lessons, which are this game's insult-swordfighting practice, play themselves: the player picks one line and watches six exchanges.

**Act III** is good. Its one soft spot is the archive: the central revelation of the game, that Hob's hand is Mott's, is one item used on one file in a room with nothing else to do in it.

**Across the game** three of the nine verbs never matter, and items never combine.
