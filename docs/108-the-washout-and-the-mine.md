# 108 · The washout and the mine — the lines (the sample)

*Tyler, 2026-11-08, approving the Fiddler's Gulch design (doc 107, errata 190), then asked whether to write its lines, sample first: "Yes". That is the eighth writing exception (errata 191). This is the sample: the washout and the four mine screens, and nothing else. **No other part of the Gulch is written until Tyler has ruled on these.** Written in one voice, under doc 59 and doc 99.*

*The rules:*
- *Thad is sincere and wants things. Here he wants the other side of a hill, and then Vessel.*
- *Daylight, throughout (errata 190). The mine is lit by old shafts to the surface, so it is the same day inside.*
- *Nothing the plate does not show. These lines come before the art, as doc 105's did, and the art brief draws what they describe and nothing they do not.*
- *Nothing in the mine is solved by ear. Two passages that are a choice sound alike, and no LISTEN line says which way is right (doc 107, "The listening stays special").*
- *Nothing marked and no hint. The survey stake, the slope and the two rocks are things in the world; Thad never connects them to the survey aloud before he has walked it.*
- *Ozymandia isn't given away. The rod does what it does at the line, and Thad reports it without remarking that it works.*
- *A way is named here exactly as its hover label is to read.*

## THE WASHOUT
*The road to Fiddler's Gulch, ending in a slide of rock and mud. An old mine is cut into the ridge beside it. Grievance waits here for as long as Thad is gone.*

**ON FIRST ARRIVAL** — "So that's what Mr. Frame meant."

### THE SLIDE
**LOOK** 1 "Half a hill, lying across the road. The road's still under there, I assume, going to Fiddler's Gulch without me." · 2 "Mud, boulders and a whole pine, roots up. It's recent. Nobody has thought to put it back." · 3 "I tried climbing it. It moved. I came down a different way than I went up."
**LISTEN** 1 "Water working through it, a trickle at a time." · 2 "Stones ticking down, one every so often." · 3 "Quiet, now. It's thinking about the next part."

### THE SIGNPOST
**LOOK** 1 "A signpost: FIDDLER'S GULCH 3 MI, pointing straight into the slide." · 2 "Three miles. I could walk three miles. I can't walk them through a hill." · 3 "Somebody has scratched out the 3 and written nothing in its place. Honest, at least."
**LISTEN** 1 "A loose board knocking in the wind." · 2 "The sign creaking a quarter-turn and back. It isn't sure either." · 3 "Wind, and a crow further up, laughing at something."

### THE ROAD BACK
**LOOK** 1 "The road back to Consolation. Two days of it, and most of it held." · 2 "Somewhere down there is my claim, with a raccoon in it, keeping my place." · 3 "I'm not going back without Vessel. I've told the road, and it took it well."
**LISTEN** 1 "Wind coming up it from the valley." · 2 "Far off, a wagon. Going the other way, sensibly." · 3 "Empty road. It's waiting for somebody with better luck."

### GRIEVANCE
**LOOK** 1 "Grievance, standing where the road stopped. To be fair, she stopped first." · 2 "She's found the one patch of grass on the slide and is treating it as the point of the trip." · 3 "She looked at the mine, then at me, then at the grass. One of us is going in."
**LISTEN** 1 "Grass, torn up without hurry." · 2 "A long breath through the nose, aimed at the slide." · 3 "Chewing. She'll be here. She's made that very clear."
**TALK TO** 1 "I told her I'd be back. She went on eating, which I'm choosing to take as trust." · 2 "I told her about Vessel. She doesn't care about Vessel. Nobody here does but me."
**USE** *(to ride home, before Vessel has agreed to the rematch)* — "Not without Vessel. I've come two days for him, and a mule's opinion is all I'd be taking home."

### THE OLD MINE *(the way in)*
**LOOK** 1 "An old mine in the ridge, propped with timbers. It goes into the hill the road can't get round." · 2 "Somebody dug this looking for gold, and found the other side of the hill instead." · 3 "Rails come out of it and stop at the edge of the slide. The cart went with the road."
**LISTEN** 1 "Cool air breathing out of the hill." · 2 "Water dripping, a long way in." · 3 "The hill breathes out, and in again, through it."

**THE WAY THROUGH** *(once Thad has crossed the mine, THE OLD MINE takes him straight to the ledge; said on the first such walk, and not again)* — "I know the way now. Stake, uphill, big rock, the still one."

## THE MINE · THE ADIT
*Just inside. A chamber with three openings in the far wall, and an old survey stake by the right-hand one. Daylight behind, back to the washout.*

### THE STAKE
**LOOK** 1 "A surveyor's stake, gray with age, driven in by the right-hand opening. More or less by it." · 2 "Somebody surveyed this, once. I can't think what. There's nothing in here a man would want a boundary round." · 3 "I gave it a push. It didn't move. Whoever put it here meant it."
**LISTEN** 1 "A drip landing on its top, every few seconds." · 2 "The drip has worn a little cup in the wood." · 3 "Quiet. It's held its position longer than I've been alive."

### THE LEFT OPENING
**LOOK** 1 "An opening in the rock, timbered, going down into the dark." · 2 "The timbers are good. That tells me someone came back out." · 3 "I've stood in front of it long enough that it's starting to look like a decision."
**LISTEN** 1 "A drip, somewhere inside." · 2 "Air, moving a little." · 3 "Air, and a drip. The same as the others, near enough."

### THE MIDDLE OPENING
**LOOK** 1 "An opening straight ahead, the widest of the three. Wide isn't always a recommendation." · 2 "Somebody's chalked an arrow beside it, pointing at the ground." · 3 "I followed the arrow. It points at the floor, and I'm already on it."
**LISTEN** 1 "Dripping, further off." · 2 "A faint draft, cool on the face." · 3 "The draft again, and a drip somewhere beyond it."

### THE RIGHT OPENING
**LOOK** 1 "An opening on the right, low enough that I'd have to stoop." · 2 "I'd go in bent double, like a man looking for a dropped dollar. I've had the practice." · 3 "My back aches already, just from looking at how low it is."
**LISTEN** 1 "Water, somewhere along it." · 2 "Moving air, not much of it." · 3 "Moving air, and a drip, like the others."

### THE WAY OUT
**LOOK** 1 "Daylight, and the washout, and a mule who's glad I've gone." · 2 "The way I came in. I know where it goes. That's a comfort, down here." · 3 "I can see Grievance's tail flicking, out in the bright. She hasn't followed me. She isn't going to."
**LISTEN** 1 "Grievance, chewing, outside." · 2 "Wind across the mouth of the mine." · 3 "A crow, outside, still laughing."

## THE MINE · THE CLIMB
*A fork: one passage rising steeply, one going on level. A shaft of daylight comes down through the roof. Pit props hold the roof up.*

### THE PROPS
**LOOK** 1 "Pit props holding up the roof. Somebody's carved initials in one, and a date, and lower down, SORRY." · 2 "I counted the props. I wish I hadn't. I'd have liked a round number." · 3 "I tapped one, to see. It's fine. I'm not tapping another."
**LISTEN** 1 "A creak overhead, then nothing." · 2 "Wood settling. It's been settling for years, and it's very nearly comfortable." · 3 "Quiet. I'm holding my breath, and I think the props are too."

### THE SHAFT OF LIGHT
**LOOK** 1 "Daylight, coming down a shaft in the roof in one straight bar. The dust in it looks like it's somewhere better." · 2 "There's a disc of sky at the top, the size of a dollar. I know exactly what a dollar looks like. I had four." · 3 "A pine needle came down it while I watched, and took its time."
**LISTEN** 1 "Wind over the top, like breath across a bottle. An F, near enough." · 2 "Birds, far up. Up there it's an ordinary day." · 3 "The F again, a hair flat. I can't fix it from down here."

### THE PASSAGE UP
**LOOK** 1 "A passage climbing steeply, with steps cut in it by somebody who hated steps." · 2 "It goes up at an angle a mule would refuse. Grievance was right to stay." · 3 "Every step's a different height. Whoever cut them was in a temper, or a hurry, or both."
**LISTEN** 1 "Grit trickling down the steps." · 2 "Still air, up there." · 3 "A drip, somewhere up there. Much like down here."

### THE PASSAGE ON
**LOOK** 1 "A level passage going on into the hill. Easy walking, which makes me suspicious." · 2 "Rails along the floor, rusted to the color of the dark." · 3 "Somebody laid rails along it, and never came back for them."
**LISTEN** 1 "A drip, steady, somewhere along." · 2 "Still air, along there." · 3 "A drip along there too. Every passage has one."

### THE WAY BACK
**LOOK** 1 "Back the way I came, toward the three openings." · 2 "I could go back. I'd only have to come forward again." · 3 "Back there the floor was flat and I knew where I was. I've given that up for now."
**LISTEN** 1 "The drip on the stake, faint, behind me." · 2 "Quiet, back there." · 3 "A drip, back by the openings."

## THE MINE · THE TWO ROCKS
*A chamber with two boulders, one plainly larger, and a way out past each.*

### THE BIG ROCK
**LOOK** 1 "A boulder the size of a stagecoach, sitting in the middle of the chamber as if it had paid for the seat." · 2 "A passage goes round behind it. Everything in here goes round it. It's used to that." · 3 "I put my shoulder to it, for no reason I can defend. It didn't notice."
**LISTEN** 1 "Not a whisper. It's very big and very quiet, and those go together." · 2 "A drip running down its side, finding the path it always finds." · 3 "The silence of something that will be here after everyone."

### THE OTHER ROCK
**LOOK** 1 "A second boulder, smaller. In any other chamber it would be the big one." · 2 "It's a perfectly good rock. It's just been standing next to that one for ten thousand years." · 3 "I've decided to like this one best. Somebody should."
**LISTEN** 1 "Grit running off it." · 2 "Water ticking on it, slower than on the big one." · 3 "Not a sound. It knows it's being compared."

### THE WAY PAST THE BIG ROCK
**LOOK** 1 "A passage behind the big rock, going on." · 2 "I'd have to squeeze past the rock to take it. The rock won't move for me. Few things do." · 3 "Dark beyond the rock, the same dark as everywhere."
**LISTEN** 1 "Dark, and a drip in it." · 2 "Quiet air, along it." · 3 "A drip, and air. The same as the other way."

### THE WAY PAST THE OTHER ROCK
**LOOK** 1 "A passage beyond the smaller rock, going on." · 2 "An easy walk past it. The small rock is accommodating. It has had to be." · 3 "It goes on into the dark, like everything down here that isn't a rock."
**LISTEN** 1 "Dark, with a drip in it somewhere." · 2 "Quiet air, that way too." · 3 "Air, and a drip. The same as past the big one."

### THE WAY BACK
**LOOK** 1 "Back toward the climb and the bar of daylight." · 2 "Back. I've grown fond of that shaft of light. It's the only thing down here with a view." · 3 "Back where the roof has a hole in it, and the hole has a sky."
**LISTEN** 1 "The wind over the shaft, faint, behind me." · 2 "Nothing much. A drip, back the way I came." · 3 "A creak from the props, back there, then nothing."

## THE MINE · THE LINE
*A junction with two passages that look exactly alike, and an ore cart on its side. The right-hand passage is the way through; nothing on the plate shows it.*

### THE ORE CART
**LOOK** 1 "An ore cart on its side, one wheel in the air." · 2 "Empty. Whoever pushed it this far gave up at the same junction I'm standing at." · 3 "There's a name chalked on it: MAY. A woman or a month. Either way, she's gone."
**LISTEN** 1 "The wheel ticking round when I touch it, slower, then stopped." · 2 "Quiet. It's been waiting a long while for somebody to make up their mind." · 3 "A drip into its bed, where the rust has made a little cup."

### THE LEFT PASSAGE
**LOOK** 1 "A passage into the dark, timbered, the width of a man and a half." · 2 "Exactly like the other one. I've checked twice." · 3 "I counted the timbers. Seven. I'm not going to count the other side."
**LISTEN** 1 "A drip, somewhere down it." · 2 "Still air. It isn't telling." · 3 "Still air, and a drip. Nothing to choose by."

### THE RIGHT PASSAGE
**LOOK** 1 "A passage into the dark, timbered, the same width as its neighbor." · 2 "I've compared the two. They're the same passage, as far as I can tell, in two places." · 3 "I counted the timbers. I'd told myself I wouldn't. Seven."
**LISTEN** 1 "A drip, somewhere down it, at much the same pace." · 2 "Still air. It isn't telling either." · 3 "Still air, and a drip. Nothing to choose by either."

### THE WAY BACK
**LOOK** 1 "Back toward the two rocks." · 2 "I could go back and apologize to the small rock. I won't, but I could." · 3 "Back to the rocks. The small one will be pleased to see me."
**LISTEN** 1 "A trickle of grit, back toward the rocks." · 2 "Quiet, back there, and getting no louder." · 3 "A drip, back there, keeping time."

**THE BRASS ROD** · USE, *at the line* — 1 *(He holds the rod out down the left passage. It trembles in his hands like a plucked string. He turns it to the right passage. It goes still.)* "Left, it shivers. Right, not a quiver." · 2 "The same again. Left, it shivers. Right, it's still." · 3 "A third time, the same answer. It has more patience than I do."
