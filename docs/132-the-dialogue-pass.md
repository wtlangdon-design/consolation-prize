# THE LAST CLAIM IN CONSOLATION
## 132 · The dialogue pass: the standard, and what changed (errata 310)

*Tyler, 2026-11-14: "Room 1 is great, but Main Street and the Nugget have already had a lot of dialogue issues, and I'm still not in love with some of the dialogue I've glossed over already too. Some of it's confusing. Some of it doesn't seem to match where the game is chronologically. Some of it's just weird/odd. Some of it seems to come out of nowhere. You decide on what comes first."*

---

## Why it happened

The lines were written one conversation at a time, in separate documents, and never read the way a player meets them: in story order, knowing only what Thad has been told and seeing only what is on the screen. Every fault Tyler found is that one fault:
- Pike asked after in the past tense before anyone had told Thad he was dead.
- A send-off offered to a stranger.
- "And that." pointing at nothing on screen.
- Fanshawe greeting a man who arrived at night with "off the coach this morning".

## The standard

Every line, read in play order:
1. **It follows from the line before it.** An answer answers the question; a question is one a person would ask at that moment.
2. **It is true for what Thad knows then.** Nobody asks about a name the game has not said, and nobody is greeted at the wrong time of day.
3. **It never points at what the player cannot see.** "That", "it" and "him" name the thing when no picture does.
4. **A joke lands on the first read.** A line that needs a second read to make sense is rewritten, even if it is clever.
5. **Nothing comes out of nowhere.** A person, place or fact is introduced before it is leaned on.
6. **And it is good.** A line that passes the five but is flat is rewritten too.

Kept, whatever the standard says: every flag, puzzle, gate and option id; the load-bearing and the silent (invariants 3 and 7); the voice of each person (doc 99); doc 59's register; and the no-parentheticals rules (doc 130), which are applied in each room as it is done.
