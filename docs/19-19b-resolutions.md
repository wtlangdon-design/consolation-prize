# 1 · THE NUGGET — draw the crowd, AND keep the dressing

The audit is right that this is the real decision and that it is larger than the line count. It is also right that drawing eleven drinkers over the "just vacated" dressing would make that dressing redundant.

**Both, and they must not overlap.**

- **Draw the crowd at the bar and the two far tables.** Eleven men. The saloon is the social hub of a boomtown at eleven in the morning and it cannot be empty.
- **The near table stays exactly as it is** — the abandoned hand of cards, the bottle set down, the chairs pulled out and not pushed back. Conspicuously vacated, in a room full of people who have not vacated anything.

This is better than either option alone. An abandoned winning hand in an empty room is melancholy. **An abandoned winning hand in a crowded room is wrong** — somebody left a very good hand and walked out while everyone else stayed, and nobody has cleared it or claimed it. It rhymes with Winnie's nineteen names far more sharply than it did, and nothing ever says so.

# 2 · ROOM 7, THE REGISTRAR — the contradiction is mine, and the spindle wins

The audit found a genuine writing contradiction: THE TWO LINES needs two occupied queues, THE NUMBER SPINDLE says there is one other man in the room. Both cannot be true.

**The spindle survives.** Ticket ninety-one in a room containing two people is the funnier and more particular joke — an absurd system running at full ceremony for nobody. Everyone-is-in-the-wrong-queue is a stock bureaucracy gag and it costs a drawn crowd to buy.

**THE TWO LINES is rewritten and needs no figures:**

> **LOOK** 1 "Two roped lines, two signs. FILING and INQUIRIES. There is nobody in either of them and the ropes are up regardless." · 2 "The ropes have been up long enough to have worn the paint off the posts." · 3 "I have stood in both, to be certain. It made no difference to anything."
> **LISTEN** 1 "Nothing. Two empty queues are quieter than one." · 2 "Nothing." · 3 "Nothing at all. There is a clock, and there is a man, and that is the whole of it."

# 5 · A RULE THAT FALLS OUT OF THIS

**Crowds are background. Individuals are sprites.**

The audit arrived at this case by case and it holds generally, so state it once:

- A **crowd** — anonymous, non-approachable, present as texture — is drawn into the background. Eleven drinkers, two queues of men, a column on a road, nine mourners.
- An **individual** the player might approach, or whose motion is the point, is a sprite. The panning man, the alley cat, the eighteen ambients.
- **A figure who must be conspicuously motionless is drawn**, never an ambient sprite — ambients approach and bark, and a man on a landing who does nothing cannot be one.

This does not amend doc 11's no-figures rule. That rule is about characters the player interacts with, and none of these are.
