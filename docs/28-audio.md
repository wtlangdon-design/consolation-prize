# THE LAST CLAIM IN CONSOLATION
## Audio design

*The last wholly unstarted area. Doc 01 describes the score in a paragraph; this specifies it. Nothing here depends on the art or the engine work.*

---

# PART ONE — THE PREMISE

**Every note in this game is played on instruments that are slightly out of tune, and the tuning resolves once, at the end.**

Doc 01 sets a global −35 cent detune that animates to 0 across ninety seconds during F2. That is the whole emotional arc of the game expressed as one automated parameter, and it is only available because the protagonist is a piano tuner.

**Everything below serves that one moment.** A score that is merely pleasant would waste it. The player must spend ten hours never quite comfortable, without being able to say why.

## The band

Four instruments, and no others. This is a town of two thousand people and one saloon.

| Instrument | Role |
|---|---|
| **Upright piano** | The town. Honky-tonk, bar-room, and a semitone flat on C-sharp specifically — doc 05 says so and the score must honour it |
| **Fiddle** | People. Warmth, motion, anything with a person in it |
| **Jaw harp** | Comedy and bureaucracy. Dry, rhythmic, faintly ridiculous |
| **Harmonica** | Distance and solitude. Always played as if from somewhere else |

**No drums, no bass, no orchestra, no guitar.** The absence is the character: this is music made by whoever happened to be in the room.

# PART TWO — THE THEMES

Six. Each is a short melodic idea, not a piece — they are reharmonised and reorchestrated per room, iMUSE-style.

## 1 · CONSOLATION — the town theme
The main theme, and the one heard most. **Cheerful, and slightly too fast for its own good** — a town performing prosperity at itself. Piano-led, fiddle joining, jaw harp on the offbeats.

**It has an unresolved cadence.** It goes round again rather than ending. Twelve years of a town not concluding anything.

*Heard: the title, Main Street, Lower Street, the Lane, the Nugget.*

## 2 · THADDEUS — the protagonist theme
**Earnest, plain, and in a different metre from everything else in the score.** Thad is not from here and the music says so before anyone does. Solo fiddle, unaccompanied, four bars.

**It is never played complete until Act IV.** Every appearance is cut off — by a scene change, by another theme, by a door. He does not get to finish a thought in his own game.

*Heard: the opening, Thad's room, and under the letters home.*

## 3 · THE STRIKE — the mystery theme
**Three notes, ascending, unresolved.** The smallest theme in the game and the most used. It appears whenever Mott, Claim One, or the fraud is touched — under dialogue, in a room's stem mix, buried in another theme's accompaniment.

**Harmonica, always distant.** It is the only theme that never sounds like it is in the room with you.

*Heard: everywhere. The player will know it long before they can name it.*

## 4 · THE ASSAY — the institution theme
**Jaw harp and piano, mechanical, in a metre that keeps adding a beat.** Bureaucracy that will not resolve into a bar you can count. Comic on first hearing and less so by Act II.

*Heard: the Registrar, the assay office, the Improvement Company, and every Liar's Assay duel.*

## 5 · WINNIE — and it is not a love theme
**The only theme in the score that is in tune.** Nine years of an assayer who writes down what she knows. It is played on the piano's correct notes, avoiding C-sharp entirely, which no listener will consciously notice.

**Four bars, no ornament, no vibrato on the fiddle.** She is not sentimental and neither is her music.

*Heard: the assay office, the records room, and the death certificate scene.*

## 6 · THE COMPANY — Fanshawe's theme
**Warm, generous, and in a major key.** It is the most inviting music in the game. It shares its first three notes with THE STRIKE and nobody notices for ten hours.

*Heard: the Improvement Company, Fanshawe's office, and the final duel.*

Each room declares a stem mix, not a track. Rooms crossfade at bar boundaries; nothing hard-cuts.

| Room | Mix |

# PART FOUR — THE TWO SILENCES

**The coffin (E7).** The music stops when the lid closes. Not fades — stops, on the nail. Three minutes of dirt and nothing else. **When Hob digs him out, the score returns mid-phrase**, exactly where it would have been had it kept playing, which tells the player how long they were down there.

**The undertaker's.** No music, from Act I, with no explanation. It is the only silent room in the game until the coffin, and a player who notices will not know why until Act III.

---

# PART FIVE — THE LISTENING (F2), AND HOW IT IS PLAYABLE

This is the only puzzle in the game with no text hints, and it has been unbuildable because the audio did not exist.

**The mechanic:** Thad taps the rock and listens. The player is hunting a hollow.

**What the player actually hears.** Each tap returns a pitch. **Solid rock returns a dull, high, damped knock. The void returns a lower note with a long tail.** The difference is not subtle and does not need a trained ear — it needs the player to stop clicking and start listening, which is the point.

**The gradient is the design.** Taps nearer the void ring longer. A player who taps at random will hear the tail lengthen and will follow it without being told to. **The puzzle teaches itself through one property changing smoothly.**

**Accessibility, and it is not optional.** The tail length is also rendered visually — the tap's ripple on screen lasts as long as the sound does. A player who cannot hear it can still see it. This does not weaken the puzzle: the ripple is the sound, drawn.

**And the tuning arc runs underneath.** As Thad narrows in, the global detune animates from −35 cents to 0 across ninety seconds. **By the time he finds the void, the score is in tune for the first time in the game.** Nothing announces it. Per doc 18, Room 33's lamp cycling ramps to zero on the same automation, so the room arrives at complete stillness and complete tune together.

# PART SIX — SFX

**No voice acting.** MI1 had none and doc 11 rules it out. That makes sound effects carry the entire non-musical layer.

**The essential list:**

- **Footsteps by surface** — boardwalk, mud, plank floor, dirt, and the mud one is squelching because Room 1 and Main Street are both mud
- **Doors** — the Nugget's batwings swing and keep swinging; the assay office's latches; the archive's is locked and says so
- **The tuning fork** — a clean A440, and it must be *actually* 440Hz while everything else is flat. It is the only correct sound in the game
- **The flat church bell** — half a step flat, struck by hand, and it must be the same wrongness every time
- **Dirt on a coffin lid**
- **The team** — breathing, harness, hooves in mud
- **Paper** — this game is about paperwork and the sound of a stamp landing matters
- **The Assay's pencil**

# PART SEVEN — WHAT TO BUILD FIRST

1. **The six themes**, as short stems. Everything else is arrangement.
2. **The detune parameter and its Act IV automation.** It is one Web Audio value and it is the game's ending.
3. **The Nugget's diegetic piano**, because it is also a puzzle object.
4. **The Listening's tap synthesis**, because it unblocks F2.
5. Room mixes, crossfading, and SFX.

**Realistic assessment, stated plainly:** procedural pixel art succeeded here; procedural *music* is a different proposition and I would not assume it transfers. This is the strongest remaining candidate for commissioning a human — six short themes on four instruments is a small, well-specified job, and the tuning conceit is the kind of thing a composer would enjoy.
