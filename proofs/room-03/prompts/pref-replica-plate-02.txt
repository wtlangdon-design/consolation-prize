======================================================================
DEFECT 1 -- THERE IS NO LANDING AT THE TOP OF THE STAIRS
======================================================================

In reference 1 the staircase at the back right rises and simply runs out of the
top of the picture. There is no platform up there.

This room needs a real LANDING: a flat wooden platform at the head of the stairs,
fully inside the picture, wide enough and deep enough for a grown man to stand on
comfortably, with a railing or banister along its open edge and a wall or a door
behind it. It should read as somewhere a person could be — because in this game
somebody always is, and he is added later.

Bring the whole stair-and-landing structure DOWN into the frame so the platform,
its rail and the top steps are all clearly visible above the bar. Nobody is
standing on it in this picture.

======================================================================
DEFECT 2 -- THE FAR END OF THE BAR DOES NOT MEET THE FLOOR
======================================================================

In reference 1 the long counter's far end (the left, stove-side end) has a
squared end cap and an end panel, which is good and should stay. But underneath
it the panelling fades into shadow and never arrives at the dirt. The bar appears
to stop in mid-air.

Finish it as a carpenter would:
  - carry the end panel and the base moulding all the way DOWN to the dirt floor,
    with a visible plinth or kick board sitting on the ground;
  - give the end a corner post or end stile with a proper foot;
  - terminate the brass foot rail properly at that end, on its own bracket, not
    running on into empty air;
  - it must be obvious where the bar stops and what is holding it up.

It must NOT fade, smear, thin away or dissolve into the floor or the wall. The
rest of the counter's length — its top, its front panels, its mouldings — stays
exactly as reference 1 has it.

======================================================================
DEFECT 3 -- THE NEAR END OF THE BAR IS TOO SLIGHT
======================================================================

In reference 2, the older version of this same room, the bar's NEAR end is a big,
heavy, close piece of furniture that fills the lower-right of the frame and makes
the room feel deep. In reference 1 it has thinned out and pulled back.

Bring the near end forward and make it substantial again: a massive counter
coming toward the viewer at the bottom right, its top edge wide, its front
panelling tall and detailed, the way reference 2 has it. This is the room's main
depth device and it should dominate the right-hand side.

Do not change the counter's angle or where it runs to at the far end. Only the
near end's weight and how close it comes.
