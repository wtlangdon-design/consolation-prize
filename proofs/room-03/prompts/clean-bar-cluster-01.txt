ONE COHERENT SALOON-BAR COMPOSITION for a 2D point-and-click adventure game in the
tradition of The Secret of Monkey Island. Stylised early-1990s graphic-adventure
pixel art at a modern source resolution. NOT photorealistic, NOT PBR, NOT glossy 3D,
NOT AAA concept art, NOT smooth modern character illustration, NOT portrait rendering.

SUBJECT. A long mahogany bar in an 1858 frontier saloon at night -- its counter, its
back bar, its stools and its brass foot rail -- with THREE men at it, each standing in
a different relationship to it.

REFERENCE 1 IS THE STAGING AND IS BINDING. It is a grayscale blocking diagram of
exactly this composition, drawn to the game's own camera. Reproduce its geometry:
the counter's long diagonal run and how far it recedes, the height of its top edge at
each end, where the back bar sits behind it, where the foot rail runs, and where each
of the three men stands and how big he is. Replace its grey blocks with finished art.
Do not rearrange it, and do not shorten the bar.

THE BAR IS THE DEPTH OF THIS ROOM, and that is the first thing to get right.
  - The counter runs from the NEAR RIGHT of the frame, where it is large and close,
    back and to the LEFT, where it is small and far. It is the longest line in the
    picture.
  - A man standing at the near end is drawn more than TWICE the height of a man at
    the far end. That ratio is the whole point; do not flatten it.
  - The counter top is 1.10 m at every depth -- about waist height on the men -- so it
    is lower relative to the frame at the far end and higher at the near end.
  - The BACK BAR stands behind the counter: shelves, bottles, glassware, and a long
    mirror. It recedes with the counter.
  - A BRASS FOOT RAIL runs the length of the bar, forward of its base and lower,
    where boots rest on it.
  - The bar's FAR END simply continues out of the frame at the top left. Do not draw
    a floating end, a fading edge or a counter that dissolves into nothing.

THE THREE MEN, and physical contact is the point of this image:

  A · SEATED, at the FAR end, the smallest of the three.
     An old bearded miner. His HIPS ARE GENUINELY ON THE STOOL and his weight is
     carried by it. Both forearms rest ON the counter top, in contact with it. One
     boot is hooked on the stool's ring or on the foot rail; the other hangs. A tin
     cup stands on the counter by his hands.

  B · LEANING, at the MIDDLE, larger.
     A lean younger man in shirtsleeves, waistcoat and watch chain. ONE FOREARM AND
     ELBOW GENUINELY TAKE HIS WEIGHT on the counter top -- his shoulder drops into
     it, his torso leans, his spine is not vertical. BOTH BOOTS ARE FLAT ON THE
     DIRT FLOOR, one foot turned out. He is turned half toward the far end.

  C · STANDING AND DRINKING, at the NEAR end, the largest, strong foreground presence.
     A heavy moustached man in a bowler hat and checked shirt. BOTH BOOTS FLAT ON
     THE FLOOR, weight on one leg. He holds a tin mug in one hand, raised, clearly
     his. His other hand or forearm rests on the counter. He is half turned into the
     room, so we see him three-quarters.

EVERY ONE OF THESE MUST BE TRUE:
  - nobody floats: every boot that touches the ground is ON the ground;
  - nobody's forearm hovers above the counter -- it lies on it and casts its weight;
  - no limb passes through the counter, a stool or the rail;
  - the rail is one continuous brass line, not a series of disconnected pieces;
  - the counter does not dissolve, fade out or change height along its run;
  - the men stand IN FRONT of the counter, on our side of it, not behind it.

BACKGROUND. FLAT SOLID MAGENTA (255, 0, 255), edge to edge, everywhere that is not
the bar, the back bar, the stools, the rail, the men or what they hold and what
stands on the counter. NO floor, NO dirt, NO walls, NO room, NO stairs, NO cast
shadow on the ground, NO vignette, NO gradient, NO texture in the magenta. The
magenta is keyed out and anything drawn into it becomes foreign scenery inside
another room. This matters more than it sounds.

LIGHT. Warm interior practical light -- lamps above the back bar and a stove out of
frame to the left. Warm ochre and umber on the lit planes, cool umber-grey in shadow,
the counter top catching a long lit edge along its whole run. Reference 5 (the assay
office) is the visual-language authority for how light and material are drawn in this
game; match its restraint, not a modern render's.

CHARACTER RENDERING -- a hard gate that has failed before.
Reference 3 is Thad, the player character, and he is THE RULER. These three men must
be drawn at HIS level of abstraction, not above it. At gameplay scale require:
  - simplified eye treatment, the same weight of mark Thad's eyes are drawn with;
  - hair, beards and moustaches as GROUPED MASSES, never individual strands;
  - restrained skin modelling: two or three values, no smooth cheek gradients;
  - restrained cloth folds: readable tonal masses, not rendered drapery;
  - hard, legible silhouettes that read at a glance;
  - NO portrait eyeballs, NO catchlights, NO individually drawn moustache hairs,
    NO airbrushed skin, NO rendered-illustration finish.
A man here must look like he came from the same hand and the same year as Thad. If he
looks better drawn than Thad, he is wrong.
