THE INSIDE OF AN 1858 FRONTIER SALOON AT NIGHT, WITH NOBODY IN IT, as the
background plate for a 2D point-and-click adventure game in the tradition of The
Secret of Monkey Island. Stylised early-1990s graphic-adventure pixel art at a
modern source resolution. NOT photorealistic, NOT PBR, NOT glossy 3D, NOT AAA
concept art, NOT a saloon concept painting, NOT a flat stage-set backdrop.

THIS IS A ROOM SHELL. Two large pieces of furniture and every person in this room
ALREADY EXIST as separate art and are composited on top of this plate afterwards.
Your job is the room they stand in.

REFERENCE 1 IS THE LAYOUT AND IS BINDING. It is the play area at 1:1 -- 1920 wide
by 864 tall -- with every element marked where it goes.

THE TWO MAGENTA HATCHED RECTANGLES ARE ALREADY BUILT. Leave those areas as plain
room: floor, wall, and whatever architecture passes behind them. Specifically:
  - DO NOT draw a card table, chairs, cards, chips or a bottle on a table;
  - DO NOT draw a bar, a counter, a back bar, bottles on shelves, stools or a
    brass foot rail;
  - DO NOT draw a single person anywhere in this picture.
Anything you draw inside those rectangles will sit behind a finished bar or a
finished card table and will read as a second one showing through.

NOBODY IS IN THIS ROOM. No patrons, no barman, no card players, no man on the
landing, no silhouettes, no figures in a mirror, no one in the distance. An empty
saloon, five seconds before it filled up.

WHAT THIS PLATE MUST SUPPLY, in the positions reference 1 marks:
  - THE DIRT FLOOR. Packed earth, hard-trodden, dry, lighter where it is walked.
    It runs to the bottom of the frame and to both sides. It is NOT boards and
    NOT planks. This is the floor a chandelier hangs over, which is the joke.
  - THE BACK WALL and the side walls: rough sawn board, studs, a dado where the
    boards change, the grain and knots of cheap frontier lumber.
  - THE FRONT DOORS at the far left: batwing doors seen from inside, with the
    night street beyond them, cut by the left edge of the frame.
  - A WINDOW on the left wall above the piano, small panes, night blue outside.
  - A HANDBILL nailed to the wall by the doors -- a small printed sheet, curling.
  - AN UPRIGHT PIANO against the left wall, mid-depth, lid down, nobody at it.
  - AN IRON STOVE against the back wall right of centre, its pipe rising to the
    ceiling, its door showing firelight.
  - A STAIRCASE at the back right, rising from right to left, with a LANDING at
    the top. It runs BEHIND where the bar will be, so its lower flight is partly
    hidden; draw it anyway.
  - A FADED PORTRAIT on the back wall, a dark oval of a forgotten man.
  - AN OIL CHANDELIER hanging over the open floor in the middle of the frame.
  - A BRASS SPITTOON standing on the dirt in the near foreground.

THE ROOM MUST HAVE DEPTH, and the layout gives you the camera: a man standing at
the back of the walkable floor is 239 px tall and a man at the front is 526 px.
Build the room to that. The ceiling and its beams recede. The wall boards
converge. The floor opens out toward the viewer. Foreground, mid-ground and
background must be three distinguishable planes.

LIGHT. Warm practical light and nothing else: the chandelier above the middle of
the floor, the stove's firelight low and to the right of centre, a small wall lamp
by the doors, and cold night blue at the window and past the batwings. Warm ochre
and umber where the light lands, deep cool umber-grey where it does not, and real
darkness in the corners. Reference 5, the assay office, is the visual-language
authority for how this game draws light and material -- match its restraint and
its weight, not a modern render's.

MATERIALS. Believable frontier building: sawn lumber, iron, brass, glass, packed
earth. Everything has mass and thickness. Nothing is smooth, glossy, or new.
